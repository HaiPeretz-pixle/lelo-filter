Is cinema in danger? The rise of short dramas

An interesting phenomenon is gaining momentum – short dramas. The film industry is at a crossroads, where it must decide how to proceed: embrace it or fight it.

מאת: חי חיים פרץ

שתף כתבה:
הדרמות הקצרות כובשות את המסכים ואת הוליווד צילום: ללא פילטר

3 Key Points Covered in This Article:

  • 1. Where It Began (2018): How Chinese platforms Kuaishou and Douyin pioneered 90-to-120-second vertical drama series.
  • 2. The Business Model & Market Size: The rise of ReelShort and DramaBox, pay-per-episode monetization, and $11B–$15B in annual revenue.
  • 3. Hollywood's Response: How traditional studios like Disney and Fox Entertainment shifted from skepticism to massive strategic investments.

You've probably all noticed the short drama ads flooding social media, and some of you have likely been sucked into them more than once.

Where did it begin?

Let's go back a bit, to 2018. Platforms like Kuaishou and Douyin (the Chinese counterpart to TikTok) based in China, began encouraging creators to produce fast-paced drama series, optimized for vertical mobile viewing.

The concept, in short, is that each episode lasts between 90 and 120 seconds, and at the end of every episode, you scroll down to the next one, similar to Instagram Reels or TikTok.

The Turning Point and the Business Model

In China, the market became professionalized and turned into a massive industry. Down the road, studios like ReelShort and DramaBox were established, targeting an international audience.

Studios like ReelShort and DramaBox brought the pay-per-episode model to global audiences ReelShort

Later on, these platforms shifted to a pay-per-episode model, where the first few episodes are free, but right at the climax of the drama, you have to start paying for every additional episode.

Following massive exposure and success with international audiences, they began producing English-language productions as well, with the most popular genres being revenge stories, mysterious billionaires, werewolves, and more.

According to data from the research firm Omdia, the short drama market generates between $11 billion and $15 billion annually.

What will the traditional film industries do?

Their initial reaction to the success of short dramas was pessimistic, thinking it was just a passing trend – but this assumption quickly blew up in their faces.

Once they realized their mistake, giant studios and television networks began investing massive amounts of capital into the field, aiming not to be left behind.

Disney began integrating the company DramaBox into its accelerator program, and started implementing a vertical discovery feed on Disney+. Fox Entertainment made a strategic investment in the production company Holywater, and other major companies followed suit.

“Microdramas are redefining what premium storytelling means in the digital era.”

— Rua Aguete, Senior Researcher at Omdia

Is the future of cinema in danger?

Probably not, but ultimately the industry is undergoing a metamorphosis – cinema is still alive and kicking, but it definitely needs to learn how to dance to the new beat dictated by the small screen.
